# 1926 census, tables i and j: towns

**i) Population européenne et indigène des cinq plus grandes villes**; **j) Population européenne
et indigène des villes comptant 1° plus de 10.000 habitants, 2° de 5.000 à 10.000 habitants**
*European and indigenous population of the five largest towns (i), and of all towns with more
than 10,000 and with 5,000–10,000 inhabitants (j), 1926 census.*

Source: sgt1926, `bpt6k9105650k` view 577 (printed p. 13). Reprinted in sgt1927 (view 41),
sgt1928 (565), sgt1929 (41), sgt1930 (571).

Rows: 23 towns × 8 groups (6 European nationalities, Muslims, Jews) = 184. Table i repeats
rows 1–5 of table j (Tunis, Sfax, Sousse, Bizerte, Kairouan) with identical values, so it is
stored once, as `in_table_i = 1`. `size_class` = `over_10000` / `5000_to_10000`.
`controle_civil` is the printed *Ressort du contrôle civil de*.

## Method and validation
Transcribed from the full-resolution scans of all five printings, read side by side.
Row checks (Europeans, indigenous, total) for 23 towns: 69 checks, 68 pass. The failure is a
misprint kept as printed: **Zarzis, Italians printed 9** (all five printings), where table k
prints 19 and the printed European total (168) requires 19; flagged `total_mismatch`.
All 23 towns are communes in table k; 22 agree with it cell for cell, the exception being Zarzis.

## Reprint comparison
All five printings identical, except **sgt1930: Ksour-Essaf, indigenous total printed 8.903**
(others 8.930; Muslims 8.930 + Jews 0).
